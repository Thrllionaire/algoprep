Pick the Family, Cold

No new technique. Lessons 0021 to 0024 are published but not yet worked through, and the mission (naming the family in two minutes) is a recognition skill. So this lesson is eight problem statements, mixed, with the lessons they came from hidden until you answer.

How to use it: do not reopen the earlier lessons first — reopening measures fluency, not retention. For each question, say the elimination out loud (what do the constraints rule out?) before looking at the options. Anything you miss, re-read that one lesson and redo this page tomorrow; the quiz reshuffles.

Practice — the decisions

Commit to an answer before revealing the options.

Saying it out loud

"Read the constraints first: n = 10^5 means O(n log n), so quadratic
 scans are out. Then the output shape: one number modulo a prime is DP,
 an ordering or enumeration is not. Then the tell: nearest larger to the
 right is a monotonic stack; many words sharing prefixes is a trie;
 cheapest weighted path is Dijkstra; duplicate substrings is binary
 search plus hash; a question about the string's own period is the
 prefix function. And before any greedy, I try to break it on three
 or four tiny inputs."
